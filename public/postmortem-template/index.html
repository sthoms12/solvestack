THE TEMPLATE

Five sections that produce real preventive work.

The worked example below is illustrative — not a real incident. It shows how each section reads when filled in.

SECTION 01

Incident summary and user impact

State what broke, who felt it, how long it lasted, and how service was restored. Keep it plain enough for non-technical stakeholders to understand in 30 seconds.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLEInternal billing portal returned HTTP 502 for 38 minutes (14:04–14:42 UTC), affecting roughly 85 finance staff. Service restored by rolling back reverse-proxy config change #412. Not a real incident.
SECTION 02

Factual timeline

Record exact timestamps (with timezone) for trigger, first user report, alert fire, mitigation attempt, and verified recovery. Separate observed facts from interpretations.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE14:03 Config deployed; 14:04 502 errors began; 14:11 Help desk ticket opened (monitoring alert missed due to wrong health endpoint); 14:35 Rollback initiated; 14:42 Verified clean. Not a real incident.
SECTION 03

Detection and response review

Capture what went well, what slowed diagnosis, and where monitoring or runbooks failed to give early warning.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLERollback procedure worked cleanly once the bad commit was found; however, synthetic checks hit /healthz instead of /api/status, delaying detection by 7 minutes. Not a real incident.
SECTION 04

Contributing factors (system focus)

List the technical and process conditions that allowed the failure — never "human error." Ask why the system let a single mistake cause an outage.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE1) Proxy config lacked automated syntax/upstream validation in CI. 2) Health check did not verify backend upstream connectivity. Not a real incident.
SECTION 05

Follow-up actions with owners and due dates

Every action item must have one named owner, a concrete deliverable, a due date, and a ticket link. Unowned actions never ship.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLEAdd proxy validation step to CI (Platform team, due Friday); point synthetic monitor at /api/status (SRE team, due Tuesday). Not a real incident.

QUESTIONS

Blameless postmortems, answered briefly.

What makes a postmortem blameless?

A blameless postmortem focuses on system conditions, missing guardrails, unclear runbooks, and detection gaps rather than blaming the individual who ran a command. People act rationally based on the information and tools available at the time.

When should a postmortem be written after an outage?

Draft the timeline and facts within 24 to 48 hours while logs and chat transcripts are fresh, then hold the review meeting within one week so follow-up actions enter normal sprint or ops planning.

Is the worked example on this page a real incident?

No. The example on this page is illustrative — not a real incident from SolveStack or any customer. It shows how each postmortem section reads when completed.
