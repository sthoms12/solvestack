THE TEMPLATE

Five checks from physical link to WAN circuit.

The worked example below is illustrative — not a real incident. It shows how each section reads when filled in.

SECTION 01

Map the blast radius (wired vs. Wi-Fi)

Test one wired workstation and one Wi-Fi device on the same floor, plus a second floor or site. Confirm whether internal servers fail too or only external internet traffic.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLESecond-floor Wi-Fi clients lost connectivity at 09:18; wired desks on floor 2 and all of floor 1 unaffected. Fault narrowed to floor-2 AP switch or SSID VLAN. Not a real incident.
SECTION 02

Check IP assignment (DHCP) and default gateway

Verify whether affected clients receive a valid IP (not 169.254.x.x APIPA) and can ping their default gateway. Exhausted DHCP leases look identical to a dead network.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLEWi-Fi clients pulled 169.254.x.x addresses; DHCP server showed the Guest/Staff Wi-Fi scope at full exhaustion after a large all-hands event. Not a real incident.
SECTION 03

Test IP routing vs. DNS resolution

Ping an external IP address (e.g. 1.1.1.1) and run a DNS query. If IP traffic passes while names fail, switch straight to the DNS checklist.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLEPing to 1.1.1.1 succeeded at 9 ms; nslookup timed out against internal resolver 10.10.0.5. WAN link confirmed healthy. Not a real incident.
SECTION 04

Check switch uplinks, PoE, and recent changes

Inspect core/access switch port errors, PoE power draw on APs, firewall HA failover status, and any ACL or firmware changes deployed in the past 24 hours.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLEAccess switch SW-2B showed spanning-tree port block on uplink Gi1/0/48 after a patch cable swap at 09:15. Not a real incident.
SECTION 05

Verify WAN circuit and ISP escalation bundle

Check WAN interface link light, BGP/PPPoE session state, and failover circuit behavior. Escalate to the ISP with circuit ID, firewall WAN logs, and traceroute output.

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLEPrimary fiber WAN down with LOS alarm; SD-WAN failed over to LTE backup within 12 seconds; ISP ticket opened with circuit ID and outage timestamp. Not a real incident.

QUESTIONS

Network outages, answered briefly.

What should you check first during a network or Wi-Fi outage?

Establish the blast radius first: compare wired vs. Wi-Fi, one floor/switch vs. the whole building, and internal apps vs. public internet. That single comparison immediately narrows the fault to APs, a switch/VLAN, DNS, or the WAN circuit.

How do you separate a DNS issue from a full internet outage?

Ping or curl a public IP address directly (such as 1.1.1.1) and then test a hostname lookup. If the IP responds but hostnames fail, the WAN circuit is up and the fault is DNS.

No. The example on this page is illustrative — not a real incident from SolveStack or any customer.
